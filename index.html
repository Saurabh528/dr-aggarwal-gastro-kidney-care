Why one clinic

The liver and kidneys are closely linked. Your care should be too.

Many conditions affect both organs at once. With a gastroenterologist and a nephrologist working side by side, you get one plan instead of two separate opinions.

  • Liver disease and kidney functionAdvanced liver disease can affect the kidneys, so both are monitored together.
  • Diabetes and metabolic healthFatty liver and kidney disease share the same risk factors, so they're screened together.
  • Medicines that are safe for both organsEvery prescription is checked against both your liver and your kidney function.
  • Transplant pathwaysCoordinated evaluation for liver and kidney transplant candidates.

Before your visit

Frequently asked questions

How do I book an appointment?

Call or WhatsApp 84278-74854. Tell us which doctor you'd like to see, or describe your problem and we'll help you choose.

What should I bring to my first consultation?

Bring all previous reports (blood tests, ultrasound, CT/MRI, endoscopy), discharge summaries, and a list of every medicine you take, or the medicine strips themselves.

Can I see both doctors in one visit?

Yes. Both specialists practise at the same clinic. If your condition involves both the liver or gut and the kidneys, mention it when booking and we'll try to schedule both consultations together.

Do I need to fast before my appointment?

Not for a regular consultation. If a test or procedure is planned, we'll give you specific instructions when you book.

I have diabetes or high BP. Should I get my kidneys checked?

Yes. Diabetes and hypertension are the leading causes of kidney disease, and early stages often have no symptoms. A simple urine and blood test can detect problems early.

Is fatty liver serious?

It's very common and often reversible, but if ignored it can progress to scarring (fibrosis) and cirrhosis. Early assessment helps you know where you stand.
